## Slide 1: The blind spot (title)

**On the slide:** Blind Spots: where NASA's microgravity fire evidence ends, and what to learn next. Full-width still of the
oxygen x pressure map: every ISS flame test at about 1 atm, the exploration-cabin target far away.

**Speaker notes:** Fire safety in crew cabins (spacecraft, Moon and Mars habitats) depends on flame behaviour at the cabin's
oxygen and pressure. NASA Space Apps 2026, "Flame in Freefall". Research-planning tool; not endorsed by NASA.

## Slide 2: The question

**On the slide:** Exploration cabins: 8.2 psia, 34 % O2. ISS: 14.7 psia, 21 %. Does NASA's flame evidence cover the new air?

**Speaker notes:** NASA adopted the 8.2 psia / 34 % O2 exploration atmosphere "for future spacecraft that will be used for
high-frequency EVAs" (NTRS 20140000417); Artemis habitat parameters "have not been finalized" (NTRS 20220011152). NASA's lunar Surface Habitat study states: "SH is assumed to operate at a nominal atmospheric operating pressure of 10.2 psia and 26.5% oxygen concentration and a capability of operating at 8.2 psia and 34% oxygen" (NTRS 20220000245, PDF p. 4).

## Slide 3: The finding

**On the slide:** ISS cabin: 241 comparable observations. Exploration Atmosphere: 0,
in all 20 material groups. Every test ran near 1 atm.

**Speaker notes:** Integrated records span 13.5 to 22.7 % O2 and 98.3 to 102.2 kPa. 8 of 11
documented NASA atmospheres have no comparable observation in any group, including all below 1 atm. Say "not represented in
the integrated records", never "NASA has not studied this": SoFIE-GEL, Saffire-V/VI and the Glenn drop-tower tests exist
but have no public per-test table.

## Slide 5: The machine learning

**On the slide:** A Gaussian-process model that says when it does not know: "Insufficient experimental evidence for a
reliable estimate."

**Speaker notes:** One model per material and flame configuration (9 groups). It separates knowledge
uncertainty (lack of data) from outcome variability and shows a probability only where evidence supports it. Pressure is
fixed near 1 atm in the data, so anything else rests on the model's stationarity assumption, which the app states.
Screenshot: uncertainty layer with the target.

## Slide 6: What to learn next

**On the slide:** Top research candidate: 34.0 % O2, 56.5 kPa, 1.71 cm/s; 19.3% less model uncertainty
about the target.

**Speaker notes:** Active learning ranks candidates by expected uncertainty reduction about the chosen atmosphere; the
baselines' picks, scored with the same model: random 0.0%, uncertainty sampling 0.0%, nearest gap
15.8%. The top-ranked candidate sits at the target conditions themselves; this follows from the model's distance-based (stationary) correlation assumption, because no integrated observation constrains that region. Honest validation: the GP beats a majority-class guess in 6 of
9 groups; in a retrospective test the ranking beat random in 11 of 18 scenarios (worse in
2). A computational research candidate requiring qualified scientific review, not a test order.
