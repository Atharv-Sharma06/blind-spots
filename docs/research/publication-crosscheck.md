## 2. Printed limit and extinction conditions (headline numbers)

All BASS-II tests ran in the MSG glovebox on the ISS; flow range 0 to 55 cm/s (about 35 cm/s with two restrictors).

* **Thin PMMA, opposed (Bhattacharjee).** TM Table 2.1, p.28: critical (radiative-extinction) opposed flow Vg,cr experiment 10 +- 5 mm/s at 21 % O2 and 30 +- 5 mm/s at 16 % O2 (1 atm). Text p.23-25: about 1 cm/s for 100, 200, 300 um and about 1.5 cm/s for 400 um; at "about 16 percent" O2 about 2.6 cm/s (100 um, 20 mm wide) and about 4 cm/s (200 um, 10 mm wide). The authors state the low-flow calibration "has high error bars". Blowoff for 100 um film "crosses 40 cm/s" (poster) / about 40 cm/s (Sec. 2.3).
* **Concurrent / stagnation PMMA rods (Olson and Ferkul).** Ignition limit about 14.5 % initial O2 (no sustained ignition below; ignition delays up to 230 s, up to 500 s without success). Flames survived to final O2 13.6 %. Lowest-O2 quench test: r = 0.476 cm, initial 14.8 %, final 13.6 %, last flow change 2 to 1.5 cm/s (matches PSI-25 test **O4**: 3/8 in clear rod, calibrated 14.8 to 13.6; verified). Boundary: X_O2 = 6 exp(-0.5a) + 0.0167a + 13.4 (X in %, a in 1/s; coefficients checked on the rendered page, Fig. 6.9 legend), minimum about 13.5 % at a = 8 to 12 1/s; blowoff branch linear (extrapolation to zero stretch about 13.3 % in the NCM version vs lowest test point 13.6 %). After a local blowoff, the stagnation flame could not be re-established at 16.1 % O2 or lower (NCM) and at final O2 14.5 % (TM; matches PSI test **O18**, inferred).
* **Opposed PMMA rods (Link / Fernandez-Pello).** Table 4.2 O2 ranges: 16.8-18.9 % (6.35 mm), 17.5-20.9 % (9.525 mm), 17.6-20.7 % (12.7 mm); these equal the min/max PSI-25 calibrated initial O2 of the P-tests of each diameter, which supports the PSI transcription. No sustained spread below about 4 mm/s (6.35 and 9.525 mm rods); spread seen down to 17.5 % O2 in microgravity vs 19 % in 1 g (TM) and 17 % vs 18 % (Sci. Rep.); "no opposed-flow spread below 16.4 % O2 at any flow" (Olson, TM p.73). Sci. Rep.: flame extinguished when flow dropped below 0.6 cm/s at 17.5 and 18.2 % O2 (samples B16, B19).
* **PMMA slabs, opposed (Miller and Wichman).** Table 5.1 gives initial/final O2, flow list and spread rate for 20 tests M1-M20. About 19 % O2 is where two-sided flames stop spreading and regress; 1.2 cm wide samples extinguish about 1 % O2 higher than 2.2 cm wide; Fig. 5.4 quenching velocities (2 mm x 2.2 cm): 3 cm/s at 18.8 % O2, <1 cm/s at 21.7 %, 11 cm/s at 22.2 % (one-sided).
* **SIBAL fabric, concurrent (Zhao et al.).** Table 7.1 (31 rows, test ids like GMT45-T4): quenching flow velocities between 1 and 5 cm/s, higher at lower O2; 6 quenches, 3 non-ignitions (16.4, 16.4, 16.8 % O2), 1 blowoff (T21, 1.2 cm sample, 17.2 %, 11 to 47 cm/s), no blowoff to 29 and 53 cm/s in two tests.
* **Other materials (BASS, PSI-26-era slides).** Ultem and Nomex HT90-40 did not burn at 21-22 % O2, ~15 cm/s (ULOI 23.5 %); Nomex-III burned at 22 % O2, 1 atm, 14 cm/s concurrent (ULOI 22 %). Three Nomex III tests in BASS-II did not sustain (F1-F3).

## 3. Pressure statements

* No BASS-II matrix column or paper reports a per-test pressure. Printed pressure statements: "Ambient pressure was 1 atm" (SIBAL tests, TM p.94); Table 2.1 "at 1 atm"; Fig. 2.20 "20.7 percent O2 and 79 percent N2 at 1 atm"; BASS slides "1 atm" (17 % O2 sphere, candle; Nomex-III 22 %); and for the Fire Saf. J. comparison "The total pressure is also monitored, which varied from 99.2 to 99.5 kPa in the tests reported" (TM p.38; downward ground tests at 101 kPa).
* Consequence for modelling: for the ISS data the pressure dimension is a single point (about 99-101 kPa, nominal "1 atm"). A documented `pressure_assumed_kpa` of 101.3 (printed "1 atm") with a +- 2 kPa sensitivity band, or 99.3 for the Bhattacharjee tests only, are both defensible; both stay flagged as assumptions. BASS-II provides no information on pressure dependence; keep the pressure length scale fixed (as the contract plans).
* The Marcum low-pressure blowoff work and Thomsen et al. (PCI 38, 2020) are normal-gravity and not part of the integrated records.

## 4. Cross-check findings against PSI-25 / the contract

### 4.1 Appendix A confirms the PSI-25 transcription
Appendix A of the TM prints 126 as-run rows (Bhattacharjee 22, Ferkul 4, Fernandez-Pello 27, Miller 20, Olson 24, T'ien 29). All 126 test numbers exist in `PSI-25_Science Documents_BASS-II_Test.Matrices.xlsx`, and the calibrated initial and final O2 agree for all rows, apart from the dash placeholders and the exceptions below. PSI-25 has three extra Olson tests (O20-O22, 2016, "video") that Appendix A does not list. TM Table 5.1 (M1-M20) and Table 7.1 (27 BASS-II SIBAL tests) agree with PSI calibrated O2 for every row.

### 4.2 Discrepancies and extra information (for the curation owners)
1. **Appendix A has a per-test configuration label and observation text that the PSI-25 xlsx lacks** (the xlsx has 16 columns, no observations). Labels: Bhattacharjee 22 Opposed; Miller 20 Opposed; Fernandez-Pello 25 Opposed + **P16, P19 Concurrent**; Olson 12 Stagnation (O4, O6-O9, O11, O12, O15a/b, O16, O18), 10 Concurrent (O3a/b, O5a/b, O10, O13a/b, O14, O17, O19), O1/O2 "?"; Ferkul F1-F3 Concurrent, F4 candle "Stagnation" (TM text Sec. 3.1 calls the candle test "opposed flow"); T'ien 23 Concurrent, T3/T7 Stagnation, T1/T2/T4/T15 not printed (Table 7.1 says concurrent). The observation text names outcomes ("extinguished at pot 0.35", "blowoff fan 97", "flame blew out at a fan pot of 7.0") and is carried into the CSV `outcome`/`raw_text` columns.
2. **P16 and P19 conflict**: Appendix A labels them Concurrent, but Link et al. use them (as B16/B19) for the opposed-flow spread-rate-vs-flow data. Resolve against the BASS-II ReadMe before assigning `flame_configuration`.
3. **Sample-number mapping**: Link's Table S1 ids (B13a, B16, C3a, C25, P17 ...) equal the PSI Fernandez-Pello P-test numbers (B = black, C = clear); O2 agrees for 51 of 52 rows (B15 at 18.4 % does not match P15 = 18.0; B14 does match P14). Fig. 4.4 captions give rod "diameters" 4.76 and 3.18 mm, which are the radii of the 3/8 in and 1/4 in rods (inferred matches P4 and P21a/b).
4. **O2 scale differs between publications and PSI calibrated O2 for the Bhattacharjee thin-film tests.** Papers quote about 20.7 % O2 for B3, B4, B11, B13 whose PSI calibrated initial values are 22.2, 22.2, 21.0, 21.0 (sensor readings 20.9, 20.6, 20.1, 20.1; B3's note says "Probably 20.6 O2 to start"). The "about 16 %" extinction tests match B16/B17 only by raw sensor reading (16.5, 16.6) not by calibrated value (17.3, 17.5). Olson's rods and Miller's slabs use the calibrated values. Recommendation: widen the O2 uncertainty for B1-B22 (about +-1 %, versus +-0.3 % quoted by Link for the rods), and run a sensitivity analysis using raw O2.
5. **Low-flow values differ between paper and PSI "Air display".** SIBAL final flows in Table 7.1 versus PSI-25 T'ien air display: T4 2.2 vs 1; T5 2.4 vs 1; T6 2.6 vs <1; T13 2.2 vs 1; T18 2.6 vs 2; T14 2.8 vs 3; T20 3 vs 3; T22 5 vs 5. At and below about 2 cm/s the displayed airspeed reads lower than the papers' processed value; Bhattacharjee and Olson state the low-flow calibration is poor (resolution +-0.5 cm/s). The flow conversion's below-range handling should carry a generous uncertainty, and the publication values can serve as an independent check (flag `publication_flow_available`).
6. **Printed-value differences** between the TM appendix and PSI-25: Ferkul F3 calibrated final O2 printed 0.9 (PSI blank/"none"); Fernandez-Pello P22b final CO 236 vs 239 in PSI; B16 air display prints "5 pot" where PSI has "0.5 pot"; P3a CO columns are shifted in the extraction (ambiguous); dates "6/24/2018" for F4 and T10 (GMT 175 is 2014-06-24, PSI says 2014).
7. The two sentinel O2 values already known to the contract (P22a final 0.6, P22b final 39.1) are printed identically in the TM, so the source itself carries them.
8. **Text inconsistencies inside the TM to keep in mind**: minimum O2 for opposed rod spread appears as 17.5 % (Sec. 4.1.2), 16.8 % (Table 4.2) and 16.4 % (Olson, Sec. 6.1.4.3); Table 4.2 flow ranges for the 9.525 and 12.7 mm rods look inconsistent ("0.043 to 0.02 m/s"; "0.0137 to 0.0164 m/s") and were stored as printed; Fig. 5.4(e) caption says "two sided" while the text says single-sided.

### 4.3 O2 sensing facts (ICES-2015-196, TM Sec. 1.2)
CSA-CP O2 resolution 0.1 %; not recalibrated on orbit; a daily offset from the ISS Major Constituents Analyzer is applied (example 2013-095: CSA-CP 20.9 % vs MCA 21.48 %). O2 is available only at test start and end; Miller and Wichman interpolate linearly between them (consistent with the contract's rule). Gas dilution reached about 14 % O2 (about 13.6 % final).

## 5. Takeaways for modelling

1. Flammability boundaries are U-shaped in (flow, O2): a quenching branch at low flow (about 1 to 5 cm/s for thin fuels; for rods stretch-rate based, with flow near 1.5 cm/s in the lowest-O2 quench test) and a blowoff branch at high flow. The rod boundary minimum is about 13.5 % O2; thin PMMA sheet quenching flow rises from about 1 cm/s (21 %) to about 3 cm/s (about 16-17 %); SIBAL 1 to 5 cm/s.
2. Quenching flows at or below 2 cm/s carry large flow uncertainty (+-0.5 cm/s display resolution, 1-min probe response, boundary-layer effects). Treat flow below about 2 cm/s as an interval, not a point.
3. Different material families need different flow coordinates: rods use stretch rate a = (3/2)(U/r)(r/ro)^(1/2); the project's `ln(1+U)` coordinate is a reasonable common choice but rod size changes the boundary location (larger rods have a higher O2 minimum due to conduction loss).
4. Outcomes are partly sequential within a test (flow decreased stepwise while O2 drifts); the papers' limit statements are at the last sustained and first extinguished flow, matching the contract's step model, but O2 is only initial/final per test.
5. Ignition and extinction O2 limits differ (ignition about 14.5 %, extinction to 13.6 % for rods); `not_ignited` rows must not be treated as flammability evidence, as the contract already requires.
6. Pressure is effectively constant (about 99-101 kPa); do not infer pressure sensitivity from these data.
